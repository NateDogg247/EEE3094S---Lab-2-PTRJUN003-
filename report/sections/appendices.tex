For the unloaded network, $R_P=10\parallel1.2=1.071429$ k$\Omega$ and $I=-15/(3.9+R_P+22)=-0.556143$ mA. Thus $V_A=-2.168962$ V, $V_B=-2.764831$ V and $V_{\rm th}=V_A+W(V_B-V_A)$. Solving the loaded hover condition gives $W=0.783081$, $R_{\rm th}=5.423001$ k$\Omega$ and $V_{\rm th}=-2.635575$ V:
\begin{align}
u_0&=-\frac{100}{100+5.423001}(-2.635575)=2.500000\unit{V},\\
g_3&=\frac{1+100/(100+5.423001)}2=0.974280,\\
K_p&=0.01(1.47)(0.974280)=0.014321913.
\end{align}
The reported 2.565 V potentiometer adjustment is an observation at an unlogged node/time. A permanent zero-error bias of 2.565 V instead of 2.5 V would predict $\delta y=0.065/(0.01432\times0.93)=4.88076$ m, so it is not silently substituted for theoretical hover. The isolated 5 m step initially commands 2.566588 V; the matched Simulink sequence reaches 2.564405 V.

Independent resistor errors change $K_f=a_fg_2g_3$ and $y_\infty/x_\infty=a_r/a_f$. The 2,048 resistor/drag corners give 48.79\% maximum error and 38.95\% overshoot. For example, $a_f=0.9/110$ and $a_r=(1+a_f)1.1/(90+1.1)$ give $a_r/a_f=1.487864$. Matched ratios and two 680 $\Omega$ U1 resistors give $K_p=0.00973890$ and 23.44\% worst scalar overshoot; physical retesting is required.
At the selected root $s_d=-0.0617284+j0.1402123$, pole angles are $113.7615^\circ$ and $66.2385^\circ$ (sum $180^\circ$). The magnitude condition gives
\begin{equation}
K_p=\frac{|s_d||s_d+0.1234568|}{1.6389585}=\frac{(0.1531988)^2}{1.6389585}=0.01432.
\end{equation}
